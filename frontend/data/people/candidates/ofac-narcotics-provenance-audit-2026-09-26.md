# OFAC narcotics-program candidate provenance audit — 2026-09-26

**Review only. No candidates enter the People manifest.** This audits the 1,035 individuals in `ofac-narcotics-2026-09-23.json` against OFAC's 2026-09-23 official list release and the current 514-person TRACE manifest. The list has 61 `SDNT`, 699 `SDNTK`, and 275 `ILLICIT-DRUGS-EO14059` entries. All 1,035 still have the same OFAC entry number, individual type, primary name, and target tag. A sanctions-program tag states an OFAC legal authority; it does not establish trafficking guilt, a criminal conviction, membership, particular drugs, a country of operations, or a person-to-person link. [OFAC defines the tags here.](https://ofac.treasury.gov/specially-designated-nationals-list-sdn-list/program-tag-definitions-for-ofac-sanctions-lists)

## Reproducible date route

[OFAC's current advanced XML ZIP](https://sanctionslistservice.ofac.treas.gov/api/PublicationPreview/exports/SDN_ADVANCED.ZIP) has `SanctionsEntry ID` equal to the CSV `ent_num`. For every one of the 1,035 candidates, it carries one complete `EntryEvent` date with `EntryEventTypeID="1"` (`Created` in the XML reference values), the matching program, and a matching profile ID. The XML's document date is **2026-09-23**; the entry event dates range from **1995-10-23 to 2026-08-20**. These entry dates are distinct from the current-list snapshot date. The raw CSV has no original-listing-date column; [OFAC's data specification](https://ofac.treasury.gov/system/files/126/dat_spec.txt) explains its fields.

I compared a deterministic 50-person stratum (15 SDNT, 20 SDNTK, 15 E.O. 14059) to [OFAC's annual change archives](https://ofac.treasury.gov/specially-designated-nationals-list-sdn-list/archive-of-changes-to-the-sdn-list). All 50 XML dates matched a same-date archive add/designate section containing the name. The sample spans 2000–2026, including early text archives and 2024–26 PDF archives. These are **date corroborations, not complete identity decisions**: the older archive files do not carry `ent_num`, and name/alias collisions need distinguishing attributes checked before release. OFAC's [delta archive](https://sanctionslist.ofac.treas.gov/Home/DeltaFileArchive) provides ID-keyed XML action files from **2022-09-22 onward**. For example, its [2026-09-16 delta](https://sanctionslistservice.ofac.treas.gov/api/download/delta?filename=DeltaArchive%2F2026-09-16_delta.xml) has `entity id="10982" action="remove"` for an SDNTK individual and preserves that person's original 2008-08-05 listing date. The candidate cohort has no removals in the current 2026-09-23 release. For earlier removals, use the dated annual archive plus identity corroboration; do not treat an old addition as current listing proof.

## Deduplication and limits

- Fifteen candidates match a published TRACE name/strong alias exactly after normalization. Thirty-five more match after token-order or spelling normalization and need identity review. The possible distinct addition is **985–1,020**, before further fuzzy matching and publication gates. This is potential yield, not a count ready to publish.
- OFAC's `alt.csv` contains **619 alternate names across 282 candidate records**. Another **303 records** have quoted weak AKAs in the SDN remarks. These are name fields, unlike DOB, passport, national ID, address, or nationality fields, which are identity checks and must not be published as aliases. [OFAC explains weak AKA placement](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/123) and the [primary/ALT/remarks joins](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sdn-list-data-formats-data-schemas/tutorial-on-the-use-of-list-related-legacy-flat-files).
- One pair has the same normalized primary name under two different IDs, **8867 and 13127**, with different listed birth years. Another record's strong alias matches a different record's primary name. Never merge automatically on names or aliases. The legacy CSV contains no non-ASCII names in this cohort; inspect [advanced XML's script/name metadata](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sdn-list-data-formats-data-schemas/frequently-asked-questions-on-advanced-sanctions-list-standard) before adding another-script aliases.
- All 50 sampled records have at least one OFAC address entry; 46 have a country-bearing entry. These are **listing metadata**, not person-specific drug activity or operating-country evidence. No country, address, organization, drug, edge, or photo was promoted. The reviewed profiles would have `regions: []`, `organizationIds: []`, and `drugs: []` absent independent person-specific sourcing.

## First-batch publication gate

For each proposed record, require (1) an unchanged current CSV UID/name/individual type/tag and a saved list release date; (2) advanced-XML `SanctionsEntry ID`, program and `Created` date; (3) an annual add/designate action with the same name **and distinguishing identity attributes**, plus a durable per-row archive URL; (4) search for later modification/removal and reconcile current status; (5) manual deduplication against TRACE primary names and aliases, with IDs retained only in review; and (6) a separately sourced geographic conduct claim before any map association. When these pass, wording is limited to **“Listed by U.S. OFAC under [tag] as of [current release date]”**, `status: "sanctioned"`, and a dated sanction event. Do not reuse an action headline as a person-specific allegation. [E.O. 14059](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/917361/download?inline=) permits support and acting-for grounds as well as direct conduct.

The current [People query](/Users/williamkeffer/Documents/ChatGPT/CDC_2026/frontend/lib/people-query.ts) filters `regions: []` out of ordinary map-viewport results, though name search can return them. A separate **“Records without sourced country association”** discovery path is needed before adding these sanctions-only records; it should have no map marker or inferred country. The current manifest already contains 68 regionless people. **No bulk promotion is authorized by this audit.**
