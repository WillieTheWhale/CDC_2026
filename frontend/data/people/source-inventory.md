# People source inventory — 2026-09-26

The People map publishes **curated, cited profiles of named people whose public case documentation individually supports their displayed claims**, with time-bound legal status, direct person-to-person evidence, and harm-reduction framing. A name in an official list is useful for discovery, but it does not itself support a role label, cartel membership, a drug-specific claim, a relationship, or a geographic association. Do not promote the candidate files below into `manifest.json` automatically. No precise personal locations or unlicensed images are copied.

## OFAC Specially Designated Nationals: best structured candidate feed

- **Official source and format:** [OFAC Sanctions List Service](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-list-service); [SDN.CSV download](https://www.treasury.gov/ofac/downloads/sdn.csv); [12-column data specification](https://ofac.treasury.gov/system/files/126/dat_spec.txt). The download redirects to OFAC's Sanctions List Service. OFAC says the CSV is a reference tool; the Federal Register governs discrepancies. The snapshot fetched for this audit was last modified **2026-09-23** and had SHA-256 `cf3ffced4675aa1542f616a15c285266bd0466d8d4508c08a1aa9851a639b276`.
- **Filter:** `SDN_Type == individual` and at least one exact `Program` tag among `SDNT`, `SDNTK`, `ILLICIT-DRUGS-EO14059`. These are specifically classified as narcotics programs in [OFAC's tag definitions](https://ofac.treasury.gov/specially-designated-nationals-list-sdn-list/program-tag-definitions-for-ofac-sanctions-lists). `BPI-SDNTK` (blocked pending investigation) is deliberately excluded from the candidate feed. Do not include the broader `TCO` tag by itself: it covers transnational criminal organizations generally.
- **Measured count from 2026-09-23 CSV:** 19,391 SDN rows, 7,534 `individual` rows, **1,035 distinct individuals** matching the exact narcotics tags. Tag incidence among individuals is 61 `SDNT`, 699 `SDNTK`, 275 `ILLICIT-DRUGS-EO14059`; a person may have more than one tag. This is a current-list count, not a count of all historical designations or verified drug-trafficking profiles.
- **Reuse and images:** The source is an official U.S. federal agency publication. [17 U.S.C. § 105](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html) generally excludes U.S. Government works from copyright protection; the CSV is offered for public download. This is not a license for third-party images. The CSV contains no licensed portraits, so the importer emits no photos.
- **Relationships and profile quality:** OFAC's remarks may contain `Linked To` identifiers, but those can express sanctions associations rather than a specific personal relationship. The importer intentionally discards remarks, aliases, addresses, birth dates, and linked IDs. A tag establishes only an OFAC listing under that program on the snapshot date. Every published role, country association, drug claim, legal status date, and graph edge still needs a matching cited designation release, court filing, or other reliable public source. Check removals and later case outcomes before publication.
- **Dated events and locations:** The current CSV is a snapshot, not a chronology: it has no per-row designation date or arrest events. [OFAC's change archive](https://ofac.treasury.gov/specially-designated-nationals-list-sdn-list/archive-of-changes-to-the-sdn-list) supplies dated additions, changes, and removals back to 1994, but OFAC says it does not preserve historical full-list versions. CSV remarks may include nationality, birthplace, and addresses; these are identity fields, not evidence of where a person operated. TRACE must not plot them as live or operational locations.
- **Reproduction:** `curl -LfsS https://www.treasury.gov/ofac/downloads/sdn.csv -o /tmp/trace-ofac-sdn.csv`, then run `node scripts/import-ofac-people.mjs --input /tmp/trace-ofac-sdn.csv --snapshot-date 2026-09-23 --output data/people/candidates/ofac-narcotics-2026-09-23.json` from `frontend/`. The output is a **review queue** in a subdirectory outside the manifest aggregator's top-level JSON scan. It keeps source URL, entry number, name, exact program tags, and a narrow source claim. The entry number is an internal OFAC linking key, [not an official public reference identifier](https://ofac.treasury.gov/system/files/126/dat_spec.txt).

## U.S. Department of Justice News API: contextual evidence, not people data

- **Official source and format:** [DOJ API documentation](https://www.justice.gov/developer/api-documentation/api_v1), endpoint `https://www.justice.gov/api/v1/press_releases.json`. It returns JSON with article UUID, title, date, URL, and body. The documentation allows title filtering and pages of at most 50 requests, and warns that more than four requests per second can be blocked.
- **Measured count on 2026-09-26:** API metadata returned **273,057 press releases total** and **8,542 results** for `parameters[title]=Drug Trafficking` (`pagesize=1`, `fields=title`). The latter is an article-title filter, not a count of people, unique cases, or qualifying profiles. It omits releases with other wording and includes non-person policy stories.
- **Reuse and images:** [DOJ's legal policies](https://www.justice.gov/legalpolicies) say its website information is public domain unless otherwise indicated. That does not establish rights to every attached or embedded image; use no image absent a verified individual license and credit.
- **Relationships and profile quality:** A release can directly document status, role, group, and named family or co-defendant links, but only after reading the exact text. Article-level topic or title metadata cannot assign a claim to a named person. Names often include prosecutors, officials, victims, and witnesses. Extracting 10,000 defensible profiles would require entity resolution, case-level citation, precise allegation/conviction language, later-status review, and human quality control; the measured article count cannot be converted into a people count.
- **Dated events and locations:** The API has article dates and bodies, which can support a dated arrest, charge, plea, sentencing, or designation event when the release explicitly says so. The article date is not automatically the event date. Use only source-stated country-level geographic associations in the map; a press release's city, arrest site, court venue, or mailing address does not imply a person's operating area. The API's `image` field is neither a portrait guarantee nor a rights record.

## DEA All Fugitives: allegation discovery queue, not current People profiles

- **Official source and format:** [DEA All Fugitives](https://www.dea.gov/fugitives/all) is a paginated HTML index whose cards link to individual pages and state alleged federal violations. On 2026-09-26, all 52 rendered pages contained **517 unique profile URLs**. A conservative exact-wording pass found **329/517** cards that explicitly mention drugs, controlled substances, narcotics, or a named substance; **188/517** do not contain that wording. The latter includes generic/citation-only and money-laundering-only listings, plus typos, so it is not a non-drug count. This is a wording audit, not 329 publishable profiles.
- **Dates and status:** In a purposive check of **29** linked profiles, name and allegation text matched the card in **29/29**, while a source publication or last-updated date was available in **0/29**. A DOB or media-upload path is not a source date. A card's presence does not verify that wanted status remains current, whether charges changed, or the eventual disposition. The [12-card unpublished pilot](candidates/dea-pilot-review.md) keeps source dates null and marks current wanted status unverified.
- **Relationships and geography:** The same 29 profiles yielded **0/29** direct organization affiliations and **0/29** historical operating-country claims in the allegation/notes fields. DEA profile tables can include last-known addresses, jurisdiction, and identifiers; none are imported or mapped. A separate current source and direct claim would be required for any group link, event, or country association.
- **Images and reuse:** **512/517** index cards displayed images, but the reviewed profiles did not expose file-level image creator, credit, or license. [DOJ's legal policy](https://www.justice.gov/legalpolicies) warns that third-party photos may retain copyright and says its copyright notice pertains to DOJ's website; [DEA's policy page](https://www.dea.gov/resources/accessibility-and-policy) does not supply blanket portrait reuse permission. The pilot stores no photo URL or image. Verify identity and original-file rights individually before use.
- **Reproduction:** `python3 scripts/import-dea-review.py --input data/people/candidates/dea-index-pilot.snapshot.json --output /tmp/dea-recomputed.json` from `frontend/`. This yields **12 review candidates**, six with explicit drug wording and six without, and **zero** auto-publishable profiles. The captured card snapshot is supplied because direct CLI requests to the DEA site returned HTTP 403 in this environment; no bypass was attempted. See the [audit and 29-profile spot-check](candidates/dea-pilot-review.md) for limitations.

## Brazilian Federal Police news: Portuguese-language verification source

- **Official source:** [Polícia Federal news index](https://www.gov.br/pf/pt-br/assuntos/noticias) and [open-data portal notice](https://www.gov.br/pf/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/dados-abertos). Articles are dated and tagged, including drug-trafficking and arrest events. The reviewed [2026-07-23 operation release](https://www.gov.br/pf/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/2026/07/pf-deflagra-operacao-para-combater-o-trafico-transnacional-de-drogas-e-lavagem-bilionaria-de-capitais) describes an operation and alleged organization activity but does not identify people in its opening report.
- **Filter and measured count:** A machine-readable named-person count was **not measured**. The news index is not a validated person registry, and many releases say only that unnamed people were arrested. Geographic and arrest-event facts may be cited after manual article review; this source cannot be bulk counted as People profiles.
- **Reuse, images, and relationships:** No blanket license for PF photographs or mugshots was verified in this audit; retain links and paraphrased claims only, and do not import images. Any personal or group relationship needs an explicit name-to-name statement in the article or another cited source. The Portuguese-language text is valuable for cross-checking South American records, not for automatic network edges.

## INTERPOL public Red Notices: excluded from ingestion

- **Official source and format:** [Public Red Notice search](https://www.interpol.int/How-we-work/Notices/View-Red-Notices) and [explanation](https://www.interpol.int/en/How-we-work/Notices/Red-Notices). Public notices concern wanted people and can distinguish prosecution from sentence service; a Red Notice is **not** an arrest warrant. Most notices are not public.
- **Program filter and count:** No narcotics-only count was measured. The public search is interactive and is not an open bulk people feed. A notice for prosecution does not establish guilt or network membership.
- **Reuse and images:** [INTERPOL's terms](https://www.interpol.int/Who-we-are/Terms-of-use) explicitly exclude wanted-person personal data and pictures from general reuse permission; they prohibit reproducing wanted-person information on another site and automated extraction without prior written permission. Accordingly, do not scrape, copy, or import these notices or photos into TRACE.

## Decision on the 10,000-record target

No audited source here yields 10,000 publishable, source-backed People profiles. OFAC supplies **1,035 review candidates**, the DEA index yielded **517 listings** with only **329** explicitly drug-worded cards, and the DOJ API supplies a large document corpus, not validated persons. These pools overlap and cannot be added as unique people. A realistic expansion pipeline would keep dated events as separate claims with exact source URLs and explicit legal state; use a multilingual queue of official prosecutor, court, and police releases for corroboration; resolve identities across languages and aliases with manual review; restrict map points to sourced country associations; and collect a portrait only when its individual image rights and credit are documented. Published people, candidate names, and article hits must be reported separately. Reaching 10,000 while retaining individually verified public-case documentation for every profile is **unproven** by this audit; never fill the gap with inferred allegations or duplicate aliases.
