# TRACE research questions and evidence contract

This collection adds observed country, year and drug measurements for harm reduction. The research questions were selected before fitting regressions; results, including null results, belong in `research_findings.md`.

1. How much do overlapping UNODC World Drug Report editions revise country seizure totals? Compare the oldest and newest positive published kilograms for the same country, drug and observation year. Report the matched-row denominator. Seizures reflect reporting and enforcement as well as trade, so these are *source revisions*, not changes in drug flow.
2. What share of UNODC price rows permit a same-country, same-drug, same-year, same-source retail/wholesale comparison? Calculate retail USD/g divided by wholesale USD/g only for positive values. This is a descriptive price ratio, never a profit margin; substances, purity, transaction size and local markets may differ even within the published category.
3. Where national seizure data and next-year general-population homicide rates overlap, is their within-country association distinguishable from zero after calendar-year adjustment? Predefine log(1+seizure kg) as predictor, homicide rate per 100,000 as outcome; report country and year fixed effects, country-clustered standard errors, sample countries/years and explicit noncausal limits. This is not a test of route exposure or drug-specific deaths.

Every published derived value receives a formula identifier, source-native input keys, source URL, publication year where known, observation year, original input values and units, and a row-level support count. In the SQLite evidence tables, `research_values.metric_id` connects to `research_value_inputs.metric_id`; `research_metric_definitions` stores versioned formula text. An absent input is never treated as zero. National seizure edition revisions are retained, with the latest edition selected explicitly for descriptive cross-year metrics. Later-published revisions do not constitute information available in earlier forecast years, so these analyses are retrospective and cannot be called prospective backtests.
