# TRACE research questions and evidence contract

This collection adds observed country, year and drug measurements for harm reduction. The research questions were selected before fitting regressions; results, including null results, belong in `research_findings.md`.

1. How much do overlapping UNODC World Drug Report editions revise country seizure totals? Compare the oldest and newest positive published kilograms for the same country, drug and observation year. Report the matched-row denominator. Seizures reflect reporting and enforcement as well as trade, so these are *source revisions*, not changes in drug flow.
2. What share of UNODC price rows permit a same-country, same-drug, same-year, same-source retail/wholesale comparison? Test whether those rows also match on exact product form, measurement concept and price basis before publishing a ratio. The feasibility audit found coarse drug labels can combine different product forms, so these candidate ratios are rejected. The separate `market_derived` table contains stricter exact-product values from the 2026 edition.
3. Where national seizure data and next-year general-population homicide rates overlap, is their within-country association distinguishable from zero after calendar-year adjustment? Predefine log(1+seizure kg) as predictor, homicide rate per 100,000 as outcome; report country and year fixed effects, country-clustered standard errors, sample countries/years and explicit noncausal limits. This is not a test of route exposure or drug-specific deaths.

Every published derived value receives a formula identifier, normalized and source-native input keys, source URL, publication year where known, observation year, input values and units, and a row-level support count. In the SQLite evidence tables, `research_values.metric_id` connects to `research_value_inputs.metric_id`; `research_metric_definitions` stores versioned formula text. For seizure totals, that includes every contributing workbook row, with sheet and row number. An absent input is never treated as zero. National seizure edition revisions are retained, with the latest edition selected explicitly for descriptive cross-year metrics. Later-published revisions do not constitute information available in earlier forecast years, so these analyses are retrospective and cannot be called prospective backtests.
