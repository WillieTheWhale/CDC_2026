# Drug-specific health collection (2026-09-26)

The reproducible collector is `data_collection/health/collect.py`; its verified independent shard is `data_collection/work/health.sqlite` (85,200,896 bytes). Run `backend/.venv/bin/python data_collection/health/collect.py`, then `backend/.venv/bin/python data_collection/health/verify.py`. The collector writes an atomic temporary SQLite database, verifies integrity and only then replaces the shard. Downloads and their SHA-256 manifests were verified in ignored `data_collection/work/health_cache/` during collection; this disposable local cache was later evicted to conserve disk space. The collector can re-download from URLs retained in `health_sources`. `health_sources` and `health_source_files` record retrieval URLs, timestamps, SHA-256 checksums and byte counts, including all 18 CDC API pages. It makes no filled or fabricated historical observations.

The [UNODC drug use and treatment metadata](https://data.unodc.org/sites/dataportal.unodc.org/files/2025-11/metadata_drug_use_and_treatment.pdf) describes national ARQ inputs and regional/global estimation. The 1991–2024 country prevalence span contains only one mapped country in 1991, five in 2000, 55 in 2010 and 31 in 2024; it is not a balanced 34-year country panel. Adult, youth and different age bands cannot be compared without a matched cohort definition. PWID estimates may come from city or treatment-site samples; use `geographic_coverage` and `method` before interpreting them as national prevalence. The CDC drug indicators count deaths involving that drug and are not additive across drugs.

## Tables and drilldown guidance

`health_raw_rows` preserves all 21,554 nonblank original workbook rows, including notes and table headers. `health_prevalence`, `health_pwid`, `health_treatment`, `health_treatment_coverage`, `health_regional_estimates`, and `health_cdc_overdose` are typed observations. `health_geographies` records both resolved and unresolved names; no country successor or subnational area is silently assigned. `health_sources` and `health_source_files` provide file-level provenance; each observation has `source_id`, source sheet and row/cell or its original JSON. All normalized table names start with `health_` to permit merging into the canonical SQLite database.

Useful country drilldown fields are year, substance, past-year prevalence and bounds, sex, exact age band, original method/adjustment note, source country name and source link; PWID/infection observations should additionally show injecting definition, survey geography and sample. For treatment, display primary drug counts and SDG coverage separately, with the latter's modeled/country status. A US harm panel can show `reported_value` and `predicted_value` as 12-month-ending provisional series, the affected drug ICD code, footnote, reporting completeness and suppressed gaps. The December 12-month-ending value covers that calendar year, but still has provisional status.

Only a directly published SDG 3.5.1 treatment coverage percentage is currently defensible as a treatment access metric. A national drug-specific *treatment access gap* should be calculated only if a matching disorder population denominator, same year, age/sex group and drug definition is available; this shard does not manufacture that denominator. Regional PWID infection fractions are already published by UNODC; there is no reason to divide unrelated country counts by surveyed prevalence.

## Verification and limits

`verify.py` passed SQLite integrity, file checksum, nonnegative/percent-range, source-status, zero-value preservation, rolling-period and missing-value checks before the cache eviction. The 2026-09-26 shard has 9 source entries, 27 source files, 16,114 prevalence observations, 893 PWID/infection observations, 12,826 treatment records, 2,490 SDG treatment coverage records, 534 regional published estimates, and 86,904 CDC provisional records. Three cells from one malformed youth row whose “Country” is `Would be great to submitt additional data` remain in raw rows but are excluded from normalized prevalence. Seven annex source files were downloaded and checksum-verified; source rows not typed as health observations (headers, footnotes, lookup lists, empty values, original out-of-scope alcohol SDG rows) remain in shard raw-row material where available and can be checked by re-downloading the original files.

EUDA health tables were not substituted: the other collector found the official EUDA CSV endpoints blocked from this environment. Any later EUDA ingestion should use a verified official download and preserve its observation year, age band, coverage and caveats separately.
